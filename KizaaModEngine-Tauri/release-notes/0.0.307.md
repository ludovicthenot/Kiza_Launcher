Kiza Launcher 0.0.307

Kiza now installs itself, keeps itself up to date, and lets you change how it
behaves from eleven settings pages where every control does something real.

## New

**A real installer.** The four-page wizard is gone. Kiza Setup is one window
with two checkboxes and a button. The launcher travels inside it, so nothing is
downloaded while it installs, and removing Kiza is a proper entry in Add or
remove programs.

**Eleven settings pages.** General, Appearance, Language and region, Minecraft
and Java, Downloads, Storage, Accounts, Connections, Notifications, Advanced,
About — grouped in the sidebar and searchable. Typing "theme" finds Appearance;
typing "disk" finds Storage.

**Dates and clocks follow you.** Choose day-first, month-first or year-first,
and 12- or 24-hour. Every date Kiza prints obeys it.

**Storage you can act on.** What Kiza occupies, measured by walking the folders
rather than estimated, with the caches you can safely clear marked as such.
Instances, worlds and backups are shown but never offered for deletion — they
are the only things here that cannot be downloaded again.

**Signed updates, with a beta channel.** Every release is signed at build time
with a key that never leaves the release machine, and checked against a key
compiled into the launcher. A signature that does not verify is refused, not
installed. Follow stable or beta from General → Updates.

## Fixed

**The settings froze the window.** Opening Storage blocked the interface for
1.55 seconds on a small install, and longer on a large one: the disk was being
measured on the thread that draws the window, and re-measured on every visit.
It now runs off that thread and holds its figures for a minute.

**Updating from an older Kiza could install a second copy.** The previous
installer recorded its location wrapped in quotes; read back literally, the
update went to a folder that matched nothing and Kiza appeared twice.

**Removing Kiza reinstalled it.** The installer and the uninstaller are one
binary, and nothing told the copy named "Uninstall" which of the two it was.

**A silent uninstall could report success having deleted nothing**, taking the
shortcuts and the registry entry with it. Removal now refuses any folder that
does not look like a Kiza install, before touching anything.

**Resumed downloads of the installer** were served as partial content even when
the whole file had been asked for.

## Notes

Kiza asks its own update service first and falls back to GitHub. Both are
signed, and neither can make the launcher install a file it cannot verify.

The installer is not signed with a code-signing certificate, so Windows
SmartScreen will warn on first download.
